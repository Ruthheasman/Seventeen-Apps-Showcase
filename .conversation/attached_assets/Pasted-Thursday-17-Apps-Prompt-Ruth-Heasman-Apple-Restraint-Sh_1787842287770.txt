# Ruth Heasman — Apple Restraint Showcase Prompt

Build a static, single-page showcase for Ruth Heasman's exact 17 BSV-focused
apps. This is a separate Apple-inspired mockup direction; keep the original
production showcase intact.

There is no backend, authentication, database, form, or CMS. The page is a
visual portfolio and every app link opens in a new tab.

## Overall direction

Use Apple product-page restraint: calm, spacious, precise, centred
compositions, generous whitespace, soft depth, and slow deliberate motion.
The result should feel like a premium interactive product page rather than a
brutalist poster, print artefact, or conventional portfolio grid.

Avoid:

- heavy display weights
- heavy borders and visible dividers outside the card treatment
- monospace labels or decorative UI chrome
- decorative gradients, glassmorphism, or background colour drift
- rotated or cropped hero typography
- decorative illustrations or generated artwork
- excessive UI chrome

The app screenshots are the only strongly coloured visual elements on the
page.

## Colour and type

Use a warm bone background throughout, approximately `#F2EFE9`, with near-black
text around `#111111`. Use a restrained muted grey for secondary type, around
`#777777` to `#B8B8B8`. Vermilion, around `#E2543B`, is the only accent and
should be used sparingly for quiet links, focus states, and small interactive
details.

Use a refined system serif stack for the hero, panel headings, and closing
statement. Use a clean sans-serif stack for the navigation, card titles,
descriptions, and links. Self-host font files when custom fonts are used;
otherwise prefer reliable system stacks over third-party font CDNs.

## Section 1 — hero and carousel

Create a full-viewport hero. Place a small, quiet header at the top with:

- `Ruth Heasman` on the left
- `Seventeen apps` on the right

Centre the hero headline above the carousel:

```text
Seventeen apps.
Every one takes satoshis.
```

The first line is near-black. The second line is much larger and muted grey.
Use large editorial serif typography, but keep the composition calm and
spacious. Beneath the headline, add the quiet instruction:

```text
Drag, scroll, or use the arrow keys.
```

### Fan composition

Arrange all 17 app cards on a wide, shallow circular fan across the lower half
of the viewport. The cards should read as large portrait playing cards laid on
a curved surface receding into depth.

Cards should:

- use compact portrait proportions rather than oversized full-page cards
- share a single centred origin
- arc from left to right with subtle rotation
- use large rounded corners, a restrained 1–2px coloured edge, and soft diffuse shadows
- show the app screenshot in a reduced upper image window, not behind the entire card
- fit the screenshot into the reduced window; when the source is wider than the
  card, anchor its crop to the left/top rather than centring it
- use a layered lower tab panel that overlaps the image window
- use a dark indigo, pale blue, or deep charcoal tab surface in rotation
- place a large two-digit app number at the top-left of the tab and a small
  diagonal arrow at the top-right
- use a clean sans-serif app title inside the tab
- fade and clip through the bottom of the carousel viewport

The bottom of the fan must use a CSS transparency mask. The lower card halves
should not end with a hard rectangular edge; they should dissolve into the
bone background.

The focused card sits at the front of the fan. Cards farther from the centre
become progressively smaller, more transparent, more blurred, and farther
back in 3D space.

### Tabbed card treatment and animation

The new card layout is inspired by a tactile folder or index-card tab:

- the screenshot occupies roughly the upper 60–65% of the compact card
- the lower content panel rises over the screenshot with a rounded top edge
- add a small protruding rounded tab shape at the panel's top-left corner
- the tab should feel like one connected surface, not a floating tooltip
- keep the focused card's image and tab visually crisp while distant cards
  inherit the carousel's reduced scale, opacity, blur, and depth

The tab is animated rather than static:

- in its resting state, the tab sits slightly low and keeps the description
  visually quiet
- on hover, keyboard focus, or card expansion, the tab slides upward and
  grows taller
- the description reveals with a short fade-and-rise transition
- the arrow nudges diagonally on hover/focus
- the animation should feel soft and physical, not like a menu opening
- the tab must remain readable and stable while the card is being dragged

Inside the tab, show the app number, title, one-line description, and a quiet
text action. Directly beneath the title, add:

```text
Open app
```

This must be a real anchor, open the relevant app in a new tab, and remain
visually subordinate to the title. The small diagonal arrow can also be a
real, separately keyboard-accessible anchor to the same URL. Clicking either
link must not toggle the card focus state.

### Carousel interaction

Use one fractional carousel position, clamped from `0` to `16`. Derive every
card's position, depth, rotation, scale, opacity, blur, and z-index from that
single value. Do not maintain independent per-card positions.

Support:

- horizontal pointer dragging
- trackpad or mouse-wheel horizontal scrolling
- left and right arrow keys
- momentum after drag release
- a gentle settle to the nearest card after motion stops
- clicking any card to bring it to the centre

Card expansion behavior:

- Clicking a card once pulls that card forward and makes it larger.
- The expanded card lifts slightly, receives extra positive `translateZ`,
  reduced blur, and a higher stacking order.
- Clicking the same card again returns it to the normal fan state.
- Clicking a different card transfers the expanded state to that card and
  brings it to the centre.
- The separate `Open app` link opens the app and does not expand or collapse
  the card.

The interaction should feel physical and continuous. Do not hard-snap during
dragging. Use `requestAnimationFrame` for transform updates and avoid layout
animation. Use CSS transitions only for deliberate settling, tab reveals,
arrow movement, or panel reveals.

The focused and expanded states must be accessible:

- make each card keyboard-focusable
- expose the card as a button-like control
- provide an accessible label such as `Focus BrandCaster`
- expose the expanded state with `aria-pressed`
- support Enter and Space for card expansion
- provide a visible vermilion `:focus-visible` treatment
- keep the `Open app` anchor separately keyboard-accessible

## Section 2 — app panels

After the carousel, show one generous panel for each app in the same order as
the cards. Each panel should occupy close to a full viewport with generous
vertical padding.

Use three layouts in rotation:

- **A:** screenshot centred and floating, app name above, description below
- **B:** screenshot on the right, type on the left
- **C:** screenshot on the left, type on the right

Use layout A for every third panel. Keep the panels separate; they should not
overlap or stack.

Each panel includes:

- app name
- one-line description
- a quiet vermilion `Open app` link with a chevron
- the app preview image

Panels should fade and rise gently by roughly 24px as they enter the viewport.
Use `IntersectionObserver` or an equivalent viewport-aware reveal.

End with generous whitespace and a centred closing statement:

## Images

Use each app's live Open Graph or social preview image where it is reliable.
Use local supplied screenshots or local captures for missing, broken, or
unreliable sources. Do not generate decorative replacement artwork.

Carousel images should be lazy-loaded below the fold, with only the first
visible images eager-loaded. Every image needs an empty alt attribute when the
adjacent card title already identifies the app.

## Responsive and reduced-motion behavior

Optimise first for a large desktop display, then provide a deliberate mobile
layout:

- keep the hero headline readable without horizontal overflow
- reduce card width, image-window height, and fan radius on small screens
- preserve the centred focused-card experience
- keep the tab number, title, description reveal, and `Open app` link visible
  and tappable
- keep carousel screenshots anchored to the left/top edge at every breakpoint
- allow vertical page scrolling while horizontal carousel gestures remain
  intentional

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Disable momentum, fan animation, panel
transitions, and smooth scrolling when reduced motion is requested. Show a
static, readable card arrangement and fully visible panels instead.

## Final quality bar

The finished page should feel quiet, tactile, and premium. The carousel is the
primary interaction: it must be immediately understandable, visually balanced,
keyboard-accessible, and reliable when a user clicks a card once to expand it
and again to return it to the fan. The app links must remain obvious enough to
use without competing with the card composition.
